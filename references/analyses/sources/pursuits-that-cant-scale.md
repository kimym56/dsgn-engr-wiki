# Source analysis: Pursuits That Can’t Scale

## Editorial evaluation

### Scope fit

This February 2024 essay argues that people who chase scale often recover meaning through bounded pursuits with visible limits. The argument can inform design-engineering culture—especially sustainable craft, autonomy, and resisting scale as a default—but it is not about interface design, implementation, or product delivery. Its relevance is indirect.

### Authority and durability

The complete essay is clearly attributed to Anu and openly accessible. It is reflective commentary built from observation and anecdotes, not occupational-health research. The distinction between bounded craft and growth pressure may endure, while its prominent founder examples and therapeutic language should not be treated as evidence.

### Overlap and classification

Proposed format: `article`. Proposed Areas: `career-practice`, `product-craft`. It overlaps with the small-team thesis in `rise-of-the-silicon-valley-small-business`, but focuses on personal motivation rather than a business archetype.

### Editorial recommendation

`context-only`. Preserve as optional career-and-craft context, not a core design-engineering reference. Any publication should explicitly label its metaphorical use of “therapy.”
