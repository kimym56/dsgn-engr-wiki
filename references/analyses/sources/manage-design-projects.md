# Source analysis: Manage design projects

## Editorial evaluation

### Scope fit

This Linear Method article presents design work as a sequence that can be represented in a shared project system without pretending that early exploration is predictable. It moves from problem verification and open exploration through focused feedback, direction selection, task decomposition, and continuous design-engineering collaboration. The source is directly relevant to design engineering because it treats handoff as an ongoing relationship: designers and engineers share project context, contribute separate issues, and use sub-issues where implementation depends on design decisions.

### Authority and durability

Linear publishes the article as a first-party account of its own operating method. Its authority is therefore strongest as evidence of one product team's practice, not as universal research. The complete page was directly accessible without authentication on 2026-08-12. It displayed no individual byline, publication date, or revision date, so its age and maintenance history remain uncertain. Product names and workflow details may change, while the underlying practices of validating the problem, separating exploration from execution, and involving engineering before implementation are more durable.

### Overlap and classification

Proposed format: `article`. Proposed Areas: `product-design-process` and `collaboration-and-handoff`. Proposed Collections: none. Source language: `en`. ### Transferable principles

- Verify the underlying problem before accepting a requested feature as the solution.
- Give uncertain exploration a bounded placeholder before decomposing it into concrete tasks.
- Ask for a declared type of feedback so concept and detail reviews do not compete.
- Bring engineers into direction selection, not only implementation.
- Preserve design decisions and implementation work in a shared project context.
